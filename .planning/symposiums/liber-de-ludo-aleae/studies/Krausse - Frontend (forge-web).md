
## 📐 Standard View Layout & Alignment

* Orthographic projection: Always switch the camera projection from Perspective to Orthographic (parallel) to eliminate geometric distortion.
* The "Big Three" views: Arrange your layout using standard Third-Angle (or First-Angle, depending on your region) projection.
* Front View: Show the most descriptive face of the assembly containing the primary features.
* Top View: Place this directly above the Front view, aligned perfectly on its vertical axis.
* Right Side View: Place this directly to the right of the Front view, aligned perfectly on its horizontal axis.
* Isometric View: Place a 3D isometric representation in the top-right corner to help the reader instantly visualize the full volume.

## 🔍 Exploded Views & Bill of Materials (BOM)

* Utilize UI features: Click the Explode or Assembly Sequence buttons located in the top-right toolbar of your software to spread the 137 components out.
* Draw trail lines: Add thin, dashed lines connecting the exploded parts back to their original mounting slots to guide the assembly path.
* Callout balloons: Use the Callout Figure feature shown in your top-left toolbar to assign item numbers (Balloons) to each individual part.
* Generate the BOM table: Map these balloon numbers directly to an organized Bill of Materials table containing the Part Number (C.07127.400.00.00), Material (Matte Dark-Gray Smooth Metal), and Quantity (137 parts).
* When a part is clicked show a small box in lower left, that has the critical info about the part.


## ✍️ Line Weights & Technical Standards

* Visible lines (Object lines): Draw outer profiles and solid edges using a thick, continuous line style (typically 0.5mm to 0.7mm).
* Hidden lines: Represent internal steps, holes, or hidden features with a medium, dashed line style (typically 0.35mm).
* Centerlines: Mark the center axes of cylinders, holes, and symmetric features using alternating long-and-short dashed thin lines (typically 0.25mm).
